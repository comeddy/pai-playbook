Why

Physical AI is a domain where answers are scattered

How do you run Isaac Sim on AWS? What GPU scale does VLA fine-tuning need? Is the sim-to-real gap really solvable? — The answers are fragmented across Slack threads, vendor blogs, and papers. This playbook filters those fragments through verification criteria and layers them in one place — so that opening it five minutes before a customer meeting is enough to find direction.

01

Direction in 5 minutes

In a hurry, go straight to FAQ Top 20; once the topic is clear, enter one of the 5 pillars; at a crossroads, use the decision trees. Every item is layered L0 (1–2 sentences) → L1 (one page) → L2 (deep-dive links) — reading just the top gives you direction.

Decide

4 crossroads, settled in one table

Each decision cuts across pillars — a tree, a comparison table, and a one-line verdict.

The Physical AI Playbook's 4 cross-cutting decision trees with their key questions and verdict principles
CrossroadKey questionVerdict principle
Cloud vs Edge “Can this inference live in the cloud, or must it sit at the edge?” The single most important discriminator is control frequency. Real-time safety/reaction loops go to the edge; if there's time to think, the cloud — action chunking is the bridge.
NVIDIA vs Open Source “Go all-in on Isaac, or go open source?” Choose by workload. “AWS runs all three well” — Isaac Sim/Lab · MuJoCo/MJX · Gazebo, a neutral position.
GPU Acquisition “On-Demand won't come up. What do we use?” Start with On-Demand. If unavailable or at scale, move to Capacity Blocks / Flexible Training Plans.
Build vs Buy “Fine-tune a foundation model, or train our own?” Fine-tuning (Buy + adapt) is almost always the answer. From-scratch pretraining is for the very few — for commercial use, licensing is the first gate.

※ Instance and region details are volatile — check the playbook's latest region table before deciding.
